FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about solar in Brunei. Can't find yours? Ask us directly.

Ask a question
Is solar worth it when electricity in Brunei is subsidised?

It depends on how much you use and when. Homes with high usage and businesses with daytime loads see the best returns, and net-metering credits help further. Many customers also value backup power, lower emissions and protection against future tariff changes. We'll tell you honestly if solar doesn't make sense for your site.

Do I need approval to install solar?

Yes. Grid-connected systems join Brunei's net-metering programme, which needs an application, engineering drawings and approval before switch-on. We prepare and submit everything for you.

What is net-metering?

Solar power your home doesn't use flows to the grid and is credited to your electricity bill. Credits roll over for up to 12 months.

What happens during rain and at night?

Panels still produce some power on cloudy days. At night you use the grid as usual, or your battery if you have one.

Do I need a battery?

Not to save money: net-metering already lets the grid act like a battery. A battery is worth it if you want backup power during power cuts or use most of your electricity in the evening.

Which equipment do you use?

Tier-1 panels and established inverter brands, each with IEC certifications and manufacturer warranties. Your quote lists the exact models.

Can I pay monthly?

We are arranging Shariah-compliant financing with local banks. Ask our team for the latest options.

How much maintenance is needed?

Very little: periodic panel cleaning and an annual electrical check. Monitoring alerts us if output drops unexpectedly.
